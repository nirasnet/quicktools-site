I told an AI to build me a spending app, and it ended up on the App Store: the story of Kapuklook

How I built Kapuklook, an iPhone app that reads Thai credit card statements and transfer slips, with Claude Code. The real prompts, the bugs, and the App Store link.

By 9M4ck · Beginner

This one started with a pile of credit card statements and bank transfer slips. I have several cards across different banks, a few PDFs every month, and plenty of slip screenshots in my photos. All I wanted to know was how much I spent this month and where it went.

So I opened Claude Code and told it, in plain words, that I wanted an app. I did not write a line of code myself. I said what I wanted, tried it on my phone, and said what was wrong. About two weeks of that and the app, Kapuklook, was on the App Store.

Day one: ask for the whole thing

My first prompt was in rough English, as usual. I said I wanted an iPhone app that reads credit card statements from PDF files, summarises them by bank and category, and, if possible, takes income statements too. I did not say which technology to use. I only described what I wanted.

That evening I typed "deploy it & test it for me" and it installed the app on my iPhone 17 Pro. I had something real in my hand the same day. What surprised me was that the app reads the PDFs on the phone itself, so nothing gets uploaded anywhere. That matters a lot for money data.

Transfer slips: the real problem

Three days later I wanted it to read transfer slips from my photo library, and I gave design notes in the same message. I wanted a "happy money" theme and pointed it at an app called MeowJot for ideas. One more thing I insisted on: moving money between my own accounts must not count as spending or income, or every total goes wrong.

That was harder than it sounds. Every bank draws its slip differently, and the app needs to know who the money went to, because the recipient decides the category. I tested with my own slips and found one bank's layout where the app read the wrong text for the recipient. Instead of the name of a noodle shop, it returned some other text.

My fix was to send the slip image back, draw a black rectangle over where the recipient is, and say "remember this position for other users too." It sounds silly, but it worked better than any description in words. It took three or four rounds with different slips before it stopped getting it wrong.

If you try this When an AI keeps misreading an image, send the real image and draw on it. It beats writing "bottom left, under the arrow". And always cover account numbers and names before you show a slip to anyone.

Naming it, and changing the icon again and again

The project was first called SpendLens, which is a bit bland. I told it "short, easy to remember, Thai words written in English letters is fine, cute." Later I asked for a big pig icon with the name KapookLook (กระปุก is a piggy bank), then changed the spelling to Kapuklook, then asked for a side-view pig, then swapped back to the big pig. Counting my messages, that is five icon requests in two days. The AI never gets tired of this, so I kept going until I liked it.

Shipping to the App Store

I did not start from zero here. I had already put another app (mnotes) on the store, so I told Claude "upload this app to the App Store, learn from the mnote session." It reused the same steps: the privacy policy page, the store screenshots, and the App Store Connect forms. On the night of 15 October it was submitted for review. I did not write any of those scripts myself.

Features I added because I missed them

  • Open PDFs from other apps through Share. I could not share a PDF from my company's HR system into the app because it was not in the share list, so I asked for a Share extension.
  • Split a bill with friends, starting from a real slip. I asked for very few steps: pick equal or custom, pick people, save.
  • Pay cycles. My cycle starts on the 25th, but when the 25th is a Sunday, as in October, my company pays earlier, on Friday the 23rd. So I asked for a custom start day for each month.

I like that last one the most, because nobody but me has that problem, and I would never have thought of it without using the app every day. While using it on 25 October I also found a bug: changing a category bounced me back to the activity list on some items. I told Claude exactly that and it fixed it.

Made with AI. Claude Code wrote all the code. I said what I wanted, tested on my own iPhone, decided what was good or bad, and pressed submit on the store.

Three habits I keep Test with your own real slips but hide the details before you post them. Never type a PDF password into a prompt; let the app keep it in the iPhone Keychain. And deploy to your own phone after every change, because real bugs show up when you use the app, not when you read the code.
