5 Tips for Telling an AI What to Build, Even if Your English Is Not Great

These guides come from Jubmue AI (จับมือ AI), a Thai page that teaches ordinary people to build useful things with AI, step by step, without writing code. Follow along on Facebook, YouTube or TikTok.

You do not need perfect English, or any coding skill, to get useful work out of an AI coding assistant. These five habits come from building a real family-TV app this way, using prompts that were full of spelling mistakes.

1. Typos are fine

A prompt such as "like this can quick enable, disable it, is anything recommend please tell me" was still understood and acted on. You can also write in your own language. Clear meaning matters more than correct grammar.

2. Describe the problem, not the method

Instead of guessing the technical solution, say what is wrong: for example, "my wife is not technical and finds this hard to set up". Ask the AI to propose options. In the real project it offered three ways and the author simply chose one.

3. Let the AI ask you questions

End your request with "If you are not sure, ask me first." This stops the assistant from guessing and gives you a chance to correct it before it does the wrong thing.

4. Ask it to check every step

Add "check each step with a screenshot". The assistant can then see for itself whether a step worked, so you do not have to watch every move.

5. Back up before changing anything

Say "Back up first, so we can undo it." If something breaks, you can tell the assistant to restore, which makes it much less scary to experiment.

Which assistant?

The same habits work with different coding assistants, such as Codex (ChatGPT), Antigravity (Google) and Claude Code. Start with a free option and only pay if you keep using it.
