5 Tips for Telling an AI What You Want, Even if Your English Is Not Great

What I learned building a real TV app with an AI assistant, using prompts full of spelling mistakes.

9MBy 9M4ck
4 min read · Beginner · Updated 22 Nov 2026

What you will get

  • Why typos and rough English still work
  • How to describe a problem instead of a method
  • Three habits that keep you safe: ask, check, back up

My English is not great, and my prompts show it. When I built the Peach TV app for my family, I typed things like "like this can qulick enable, disable it, is anything is recomend please tell me", with the mistakes left in. It still worked. Here are the five habits that made the difference.

1. Typos are fine

The assistant understood that sentence and built what I meant. You can also just write in your own language. Meaning matters more than grammar.

2. Say the problem, not the method

I did not know how to solve it, so I described the situation: "non technical from my wife it hard to config it". Then I asked the AI for options. That time it offered three ways and I simply picked one.

3. Let it ask you questions

I end requests with "If you're not sure, ask me first." The AI stops guessing and checks with me before doing the wrong thing.

4. Ask it to check every step

I add "check each step with a screenshot". The AI can see for itself whether a step worked, so I don't have to watch every move.

5. Back up before changing anything

I say "Back up first, so we can undo it." If something breaks, I tell it to restore. That makes it much less scary to try things.

Works with different assistants. I use Codex (ChatGPT), Antigravity (Google) and Claude Code. Start with a free one. New to this? Start from zero here.

You can see these habits in action in how I set up kids mode on our TV.
