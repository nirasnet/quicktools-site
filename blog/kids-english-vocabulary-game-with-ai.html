Make a Kids' English Vocabulary Game With AI in About Two Minutes

These guides come from Jubmue AI (จับมือ AI), a Thai page that teaches ordinary people to build useful things with AI, step by step, without writing code. Follow along on Facebook, YouTube or TikTok.

Here is a small project that shows what "no code" really means. You describe a game in plain language, the AI writes a single HTML file, and you double-click it to play. In the original test the author typed the request once, waited about one minute 25 seconds, and received a game that worked. They then played all 10 questions to check it.

What the game does

  • Three categories: animals, fruit and colours.
  • Tap a big emoji, then choose one of three answers. The English word is read aloud.
  • A correct answer makes a star pop up with a praise sound. A wrong answer says "try again" without scolding.
  • After 10 questions the child gets a trophy and confetti.

How to make it

  1. Open your AI coding assistant (Codex, Antigravity or Claude Code all work).
  2. Copy the prompt below and paste it in.
  3. Wait a moment. You receive a file named index.html.
  4. Double-click the file. It opens in your browser and is ready to play. On an iPad, open it in Safari.

The prompt (copy and paste)

Make an English vocabulary guessing game for my 6-year-old that works on an iPad.
It must be a single index.html file that opens in Safari without installing anything.
- Show a large emoji and let the child tap one of 3 answer buttons. Big buttons that are easy to tap.
- Tapping an answer reads the English word aloud.
- A correct answer shows a bouncing star and a praise sound. A wrong answer says try again, no scolding.
- Keep score. After 10 questions show a "well done" screen.
- Categories to choose from: animals, fruit, colours.
- Bright colours, large text, with a little Thai labelling.

Make it your own

You can keep chatting with the AI to add categories such as numbers or vehicles, or to adjust it for a different age. Check the result yourself before handing it to a child, and keep the file on your own device.
