Set Up Kids Mode and Screen-Time Rules on an Android TV With an AI Assistant

These guides come from Jubmue AI (จับมือ AI), a Thai page that teaches ordinary people to build useful things with AI, step by step, without writing code. Follow along on Facebook, YouTube or TikTok.

A child who sneaks into other apps and watches past bedtime is a common family problem. This guide follows a real home setup: an AI coding assistant does the technical work on an Android TV or Google TV while you answer a few plain questions. The author did not write any code by hand, and the screenshots in the original videos are from the family's own TV.

What you get

  • A kids home screen that shows only the apps you allow, with nothing else to tempt them.
  • Viewing hours you choose, for example school days 07:00-07:15 and 17:00-20:30, weekends 07:00-20:30.
  • When time is up the TV locks itself and goes quiet. No argument is needed.
  • Optionally, with a Mac and an iPhone, an app to lock the TV, switch modes and see what was watched.

Before you start

  • This works for Android TV or Google TV (for example TCL, Sony, Xiaomi, Hisense, Chromecast). It does not work for Samsung, LG or Fire TV.
  • You need a Mac or Windows computer on the same Wi-Fi as the TV.
  • You need an AI coding assistant that can run commands on your computer, such as Claude Code, GitHub Copilot CLI, Codex or Gemini CLI.

Step by step

  1. Turn on developer mode on the TV. Go to Settings, System, About, then press OK on the Android TV OS build seven times.
  2. Turn on USB debugging in the new Developer options menu.
  3. Clean the TV first. Open your assistant and paste the first prompt below. It uses the open-source CleanYourTV project to remove ads and junk apps. Nothing is uninstalled for good; you can ask the assistant to restore the TV.
  4. Approve the computer when the TV asks, then answer the assistant's questions.
  5. Add kids mode by pasting the second prompt in the same chat. It asks which apps your children may open and which hours the TV may be used.

Prompt 1: clean the TV

Talk to me in Thai. Clone https://github.com/Gathin23/cleanyourtv and work inside that folder.
Open its PROMPT.md and do everything between the two --- lines, exactly as if I had pasted it to you.
My TV model is: ____ (if I left this blank, read the model from the TV once you are connected).

Prompt 2: kids mode and time rules

Talk to me in Thai. My TV was cleaned with CleanYourTV and the cleanyourtv folder is on this computer.
Clone https://github.com/nirasnet/peach-tv next to that folder, read its README.md, and set up the family TV
on my real TV step by step, checking each step with a screenshot from the TV:
1. Install the TV agent with tv-agent/install.sh. If network ADB is already closed, tell me how to turn
   USB debugging back on, and close it again at the end. Keep the agent token private, don't print it in chat.
2. Ask me which apps my kids may open, then turn on Kids mode with only those apps.
3. Ask me what hours the TV may be used on school days and on weekends, and the daily screen-time limit,
   then set those rules.
4. Show me the unlock code and how to unlock the lock screen with the remote.
5. Ask me whether I have a Mac with Xcode and an iPhone. If yes, build and install the Peach TV app
   (ios/build-and-install.sh) and pair it with the TV. If not, give me a small script to lock and unlock
   the TV from this computer.
Ask me before anything that needs my Apple account. Skip "away from home" (Tailscale) unless I ask for it.

Change "Talk to me in Thai" to your own language. The full text and the latest version are in the peach-tv repository.

Safety notes

  • Developer mode and USB debugging give your computer deep control of the TV. Turn USB debugging off again when you finish, as the prompt asks.
  • Keep the access token private and do not paste it in chat.
  • Use this only on a TV that belongs to you, and review what the assistant plans before you approve it.
