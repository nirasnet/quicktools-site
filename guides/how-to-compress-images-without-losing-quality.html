How to Compress Images Without Losing Quality

Large photos slow down web pages and bounce off email size limits. The good news is that most images can be made 60 to 80 percent smaller with no visible change.

1. Resize before you compress

A phone photo is often 4000 pixels wide, but a blog column shows about 800 to 1600 pixels. Shrinking the dimensions to what you actually need removes most of the data before compression even starts.

2. Pick the right format

  • JPG is the usual choice for photographs.
  • WebP is usually 20 to 30 percent smaller than JPG for similar quality and is supported by all modern browsers.
  • PNG is best for screenshots, logos and anything with sharp text or transparency, but it produces large files for photos.

3. Lower the quality a little

Quality 75 to 85 is the sweet spot for JPG and WebP. Below about 60 you start to see blocky patches around edges and in skies.

4. Keep your original

Every time a JPG is saved again it loses a bit more detail. Always compress from the original file, never from an already compressed copy.
