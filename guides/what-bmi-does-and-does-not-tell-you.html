What BMI Does (and Does Not) Tell You

Body mass index, or BMI, is a quick number that compares your weight with your height. Doctors and public-health researchers use it because it is simple, but it has clear limits.

How it is calculated

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ height in metres squared. Someone who weighs 70 kg and is 1.70 m tall has a BMI of about 24.2.

The adult ranges

  • Under 18.5: underweight range
  • 18.5 to 24.9: healthy weight range
  • 25 to 29.9: overweight range
  • 30 or more: obesity range

Where BMI falls short

  • It cannot tell muscle from fat, so a muscular person can be labelled overweight.
  • It ignores where fat is stored. Fat around the waist carries more health risk than fat on the hips.
  • Age, sex and ethnicity change what a healthy range looks like. Some health bodies use lower cut-offs for people of South Asian descent, for example.
  • Children and teenagers are assessed on age-based growth charts, not these adult ranges.

Better to use it as a starting point

Treat BMI as one clue alongside waist measurement, blood pressure, blood tests, activity level and family history. If your number surprises you, talk to a doctor rather than relying on a calculator.
