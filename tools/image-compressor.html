Settings

    How to make images smaller

    Add one or many photos, then pick a preset or tune the settings. Lower the quality to 70 to 85 (the difference is hard to see but the file is often 60 to 80 percent smaller), set a max width that matches where the image will be shown (1600 px for a blog post, 1024 for email), and choose WebP or JPG for photos. Each row shows the old and new size, and you can download single files or everything as one ZIP.

    Which format?

    • JPG: best for photos. Does not support transparency, so transparent areas are filled with the colour you choose.
    • WebP: usually smaller than JPG at the same look, and supports transparency. Supported by all current browsers.
    • PNG: lossless and good for screenshots, logos and text. The quality slider has no effect, but resizing still shrinks it.
    • Keep original: each file keeps its own format.

    Also in this tool

    Rotate sideways photos, flip images, and add a suffix to file names so your originals are never overwritten. Re-saving a JPG always loses a little detail, so keep your originals. Camera metadata such as location and date is removed because the image is redrawn, a handy side effect before sharing photos online.

    All processing happens in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and your settings are remembered on your own device only.
